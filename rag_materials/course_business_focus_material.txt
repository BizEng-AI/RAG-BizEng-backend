BizEng course focus material

Second-semester business-focused themes:
- travel and transport
- the Trans-Siberian railway
- business and work
- great entrepreneurs
- basic economic concepts
- supply and demand
- personal finance and business
- trade and markets

Corporate travel planning:
A learner may need to plan a 3-day business trip and discuss transport, accommodation, budget, and purpose of travel. Strong phrases include cost-effective, within budget, negotiate rates, corporate discount, flexible booking, travel reimbursement, schedule adjustment, and estimated expenditure. Useful starters include From a financial perspective, In terms of logistics, Our priority is efficiency, We aim to minimize expenses, and This option offers better value for money.

Trans-Siberian railway and logistics:
The Trans-Siberian Railway is used as a cost-effective alternative to sea or air transport for some Europe-Asia freight routes. Students should compare rail, sea, and air based on cost, transit time, reliability, cargo capacity, infrastructure, and possible customs delays. Business language should justify a route choice, identify risks, and suggest mitigation steps such as document checks, schedule buffers, and clear client updates.

International meetings and negotiation:
Students should be able to open a meeting formally, confirm an agenda, present a proposal, respond with polite disagreement, make a counteroffer, offer a concession, and summarize a final agreement. Useful phrases include Shall we begin, I see your point, however, From our perspective, Would you consider, and We are willing to proceed provided that. Important vocabulary includes counteroffer, compromise, proposal, agenda, stakeholder, mutual benefit, reach consensus, concession, contract terms, and final agreement.

Follow-up email practice:
After a meeting or negotiation, the learner should summarize the meeting purpose, main discussion points, final agreement, action owners, deadlines, and next steps in a clear professional email.

Economics and finance:
Students should explain goods and services, inflation, supply and demand, shortages, pricing, salary, monthly budgets, personal expenses, and trade between buyers and sellers. The language level should stay practical and clear rather than overly technical.
